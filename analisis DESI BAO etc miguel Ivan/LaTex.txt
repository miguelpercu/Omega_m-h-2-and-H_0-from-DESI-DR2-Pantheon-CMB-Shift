\begin{abstract}
We present a phenomenological measurement of the cosmological parameters $(H_0, \Omega_m h^2)$ using three independent datasets: DESI DR2 baryon acoustic oscillation measurements, the Pantheon+ Type Ia supernova sample with full covariance, and Cosmic Microwave Background shift parameters. The analysis is performed with an MCMC sampler under the assumption of a spatially flat $\Lambda$CDM background. We obtain $H_0 = 69.91 \pm 0.71$ km/s/Mpc and $\Omega_m h^2 = 0.1476 \pm 0.0013$. The value of $\Omega_m h^2$ is $3.8\%$ higher than the Planck 2018 value ($0.1422 \pm 0.0008$) at approximately $3.6\sigma$, a discrepancy that connects to the well-documented tension between early-universe probes (CMB) and late-universe probes (BAO + SNe). The result is a measurement of the matter density within $\Lambda$CDM and is not interpreted here as evidence for any extension beyond it. The full MCMC chain, data files, and analysis scripts are publicly available.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The value of the matter density parameter $\Omega_m h^2$ is one of the most tightly constrained quantities in cosmology. It is measured independently by:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) power spectrum, primarily through the acoustic peak structure;
    \item Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO) in the galaxy distribution;
    \item Type Ia Supernovae (SNe Ia) as standardizable candles.
\end{itemize}

Recent data releases ---DESI DR2 for BAO \cite{DESI2024} and Pantheon+ for SNe Ia \cite{Brout2022}--- have reduced statistical uncertainties to the percent level. This precision makes it possible to test the internal consistency of the flat $\Lambda$CDM model across different observables.

We present here a joint MCMC analysis of DESI DR2 + Pantheon+ + CMB shift parameters. The goal is to measure $(H_0, \Omega_m h^2)$ and compare with the Planck 2018 value. This is a standard phenomenological exercise; no extended framework is assumed or tested.

\section{Data}

\subsection{DESI DR2 BAO}

We use the 13 BAO measurements from DESI DR2 \cite{DESI2024}, which include $D_M/r_d$, $D_H/r_d$, and $D_V/r_d$ at multiple effective redshifts between $z = 0.3$ and $z = 2.33$. The full $13 \times 13$ covariance matrix is used.

\subsection{Pantheon+ SNe Ia}

The Pantheon+ sample contains 1701 SNe Ia \cite{Brout2022}. We use the subset with $z > 0.01$ (1590 SNe) and the full $1701 \times 1701$ statistical plus systematic covariance matrix, restricted to the selected subset. The distance modulus is modeled as:

\begin{equation}
\mu(z) = 5 \log_{10}\left[(1+z) D_M(z)\right] + 25,
\end{equation}

and the likelihood includes an analytic marginalization over the absolute magnitude $M$.

\subsection{CMB shift parameters}

We use the two standard CMB shift parameters:

\begin{equation}
l_A = \pi \frac{D_M(z_*)}{r_s(z_*)}, \qquad R = \sqrt{\Omega_m} \frac{H_0 D_M(z_*)}{c},
\end{equation}

with Planck 2018 values $l_A = 301.47 \pm 0.09$ and $R = 1.750 \pm 0.005$ \cite{Planck2020}. Throughout the analysis, the recombination and drag redshifts are held fixed at $z_* = 1090$ and $z_{\rm drag} = 1059.94$, respectively, as commonly adopted in BAO and CMB shift analyses. We do not use the Hu \& Sugiyama fitting formula for the transfer function; instead, the sound horizon $r_d$ is computed by direct numerical integration of the comoving sound speed, which is numerically stable for the flat $\Lambda$CDM background considered here.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Cosmological model}

We assume a spatially flat $\Lambda$CDM universe:

\begin{equation}
E^2(z) = \Omega_m(1+z)^3 + \Omega_r(1+z)^4 + \Omega_\Lambda,
\end{equation}

with $\Omega_\Lambda = 1 - \Omega_m - \Omega_r$ and $\Omega_r$ fixed to $9.15 \times 10^{-5}$.

The free parameters are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $H_0$ [km/s/Mpc]
    \item $\Omega_m h^2$, where $h = H_0/100$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Likelihood}

The total log-likelihood is:

\begin{equation}
-2 \ln \mathcal{L} = \chi^2_{\mathrm{SN}} + \chi^2_{\mathrm{BAO}} + \chi^2_{\mathrm{CMB}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{MCMC and priors}

We sample the posterior with \texttt{emcee} \cite{ForemanMackey2013}, using 32 walkers and 2500 steps, discarding 700 as burn-in. The flat priors adopted for the free parameters are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $55 < H_0 < 85$ km/s/Mpc;
    \item $0.10 < \Omega_m h^2 < 0.20$.
\end{itemize}

These ranges are deliberately broad, encompassing the Planck and SH0ES determinations as well as the intermediate values reported in combined analyses. The integrated autocorrelation times give effective sample sizes of $\mathrm{ESS}(H_0) \approx 2385$ and $\mathrm{ESS}(\Omega_m h^2) \approx 2396$, ensuring converged chains.

To verify statistical stability, we performed four independent MCMC runs 
with different random seeds (42, 123, 2024, 7). All runs produce values 
of $H_0$ and $\Omega_m h^2$ within $1\sigma$ of the baseline (seed 42), 
with deviations $\Delta H_0 \leq 0.03\sigma$ and $\Delta \Omega_m h^2 \leq 0.02\sigma$. 
The scatter across seeds ($\sigma(H_0) = 0.011$ km/s/Mpc, 
$\sigma(\Omega_m h^2) = 1.6 \times 10^{-5}$) is more than one order of 
magnitude smaller than the individual parameter uncertainties, confirming 
both convergence and reproducibility.

\section{Results}

The marginalized constraints are:

\begin{equation}
H_0 = 69.91 \pm 0.71 \text{ km/s/Mpc},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Omega_m h^2 = 0.14763 \pm 0.00126.
\end{equation}

The best-fit $\chi^2$ is $1420.53$ for $N = 1605$ data points (13 BAO + 1590 SNe + 2 CMB), giving a reduced $\chi^2$ of $\chi^2/N \approx 0.885$. A reduced $\chi^2$ below unity in a joint cosmological analysis is common and typically indicates that the Pantheon+ statistical plus systematic covariance dominates the total $\chi^2$ budget, absorbing residual scatter that would otherwise appear as an excess. It is not interpreted here as evidence of overfitting.

\subsection{Deviation from Planck}

The measured $\Omega_m h^2 = 0.14763 \pm 0.00126$ differs from the Planck 2018 value $\Omega_m h^2 = 0.14222 \pm 0.00083$ by:

\begin{equation}
\Delta (\Omega_m h^2) = +0.00541 \pm 0.00151 \quad (\sim 3.6\sigma).
\end{equation}

This deviation is consistent in direction and magnitude with recent reports of tension between early-universe probes (CMB) and late-universe probes (BAO + SNe). It is not interpreted here as evidence for any extension beyond $\Lambda$CDM; it is a measurement of the matter density using three independent datasets. The $H_0$ value obtained here ($69.91 \pm 0.71$) lies between the Planck value ($67.4$) and the SH0ES value ($73.0$), in agreement with other combined analyses.

\subsection{Comparison with Recent Literature}

Our measured value of $\Omega_m h^2$ is higher than the Planck 2018 result, in qualitative agreement with recent analyses of DESI DR2 data that have reported tensions in the matter density parameter. A study using DESI DR2 BAO combined with DES Y6 angular BAO found consistency with both Planck and DESI DR2 determinations, and quantified the tension in $\Omega_m$ at approximately $1.84\sigma$ between low and high redshift bins \cite{Chaudhary2025}. Another analysis using Gaussian process regression on DESI DR2 + Pantheon+ found $H_0 = 69.29 \pm 0.81$ km/s/Mpc, occupying an intermediate position between Planck and SH0ES \cite{Jia2025}. Our result for $H_0 = 69.91 \pm 0.71$ km/s/Mpc is consistent with this intermediate range.

The tension we observe in $\Omega_m h^2$ ($\sim 3.6\sigma$) is larger than the $\sim 1.8\sigma$ reported between DESI DR2 redshift bins \cite{Chaudhary2025}, but is consistent with the broader pattern of tensions reported when combining DESI BAO, CMB, and SNe Ia data \cite{Farren2025}. The origin of these tensions remains an open question, with proposed explanations including dynamical dark energy, modified gravity, or unknown systematics in specific tracers \cite{Chaudhary2025,HowMuch2026}.

\section{Conclusion}

We have presented a phenomenological measurement of $H_0$ and $\Omega_m h^2$ using DESI DR2, Pantheon+, and CMB shift parameters. The result:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $H_0 = 69.91 \pm 0.71$ km/s/Mpc;
    \item $\Omega_m h^2 = 0.14763 \pm 0.00126$;
    \item $\sim 3.6\sigma$ deviation from Planck 2018 in $\Omega_m h^2$;
    \item $H_0$ intermediate between Planck and SH0ES.
\end{itemize}

The full MCMC chain, data, and analysis scripts are available at the accompanying repository. The result is a measurement of standard cosmological parameters and does not require or imply any framework beyond $\Lambda$CDM.
